Key Glossary Terms

Diagram assumptions & definitions

This diagram illustrates negative externalities in production: MSC = MPC + MEC, with no external benefits (MSB = MPB).

MPB: the additional benefit to the consumer from consuming an additional unit. MPC: the additional cost to the producer of producing an additional unit.

MPB (Marginal Private Benefit)

The additional benefit enjoyed by the private consumer from consuming an extra unit of a good or service.

MPC (Marginal Private Cost)

The additional cost incurred by the private producer from producing an extra unit of a good or service.

MEC (Marginal External Cost)

The uncompensated cost inflicted on third parties outside the market transaction from producing one extra unit.

MEB (Marginal External Benefit)

The uncompensated gain enjoyed by third parties outside the market transaction from producing or consuming one extra unit. (Note: external benefits are assumed absent in this negative-production-externality diagram, so MPB = MSB).

MSC (Marginal Social Cost)

The total cost to society of producing an extra unit of output (MSC = MPC + MEC).

MSB (Marginal Social Benefit)

The total benefit to society of consuming an extra unit of output (MSB = MPB + MEB).

Deadweight Loss (DWL)

The reduction in net benefit to society when output level is not at the social optimum (represented by triangle AEsEe).

Free Market Equilibrium (Pe, Qe)

The price and quantity determined solely by private market forces at point Ee where private demand equals private supply (MPB = MPC), ignoring external spillover costs.

Socially Optimal Equilibrium (Ps, Qs)

The allocatively efficient point Es where social welfare is maximized (MSB = MSC).

Over-production / Over-consumption

Occurs when market output exceeds the socially desirable level (Qe > Qs). This is driven by negative externalities in production (over-production) or consumption (over-consumption).
