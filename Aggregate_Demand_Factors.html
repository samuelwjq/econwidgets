Glossary

Aggregate Demand (AD)

Total planned expenditure on domestically produced final goods and services at each general price level over a period of time. AD = C + I + G + (X − M).

Aggregate Supply (AS)

Total output that producers in an economy are willing and able to supply at each general price level over a period of time. This widget uses a Keynesian AS curve with horizontal, upward-sloping and vertical sections.

Consumption (C)

Household expenditure on goods and services.

Investment (I)

Expenditure on capital goods and additions to inventories; purchases of existing financial assets are excluded.

Government Expenditure (G)

Government purchases of goods and services. Transfer payments are excluded because they are not payment for current production.

Exports (X)

Expenditure by foreign buyers on domestically produced goods and services.

Imports (M)

Expenditure on foreign-produced goods and services. Imports are subtracted because imported expenditure may already be included in C, I and G and is not demand for domestic output.

Net Exports (X − M)

Export expenditure minus import expenditure. Holding other components constant, higher X or lower M increases net exports and AD; lower X or higher M reduces them.

Change in Aggregate Demand

A change in planned expenditure at every general price level due to a change in a non-price determinant, represented by a shift of the AD curve, ceteris paribus.

Change in Real Output Demanded

A change in real output demanded caused by a change in the general price level, represented by a movement along an unchanged AD curve, ceteris paribus.

Full-Employment Output

The level of real output produced when available resources are fully employed. The vertical section of the Keynesian AS curve represents this capacity constraint.
