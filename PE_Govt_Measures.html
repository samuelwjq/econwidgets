Key Concepts & Policies

Diagram assumptions & definitions

This diagram illustrates positive externalities in consumption: MSB = MPB + MEB, with no external costs (MSC = MPC).

MPB: the additional benefit to the consumer from consuming an additional unit. MPC: the additional cost to the producer of producing an additional unit.

Deadweight Loss (DWL)

DWL is the reduction in net benefit to society because output is not at the socially optimal level where MSB = MSC.

Market Equilibrium (Ee / Qe, Pe)

The output level (Qe) and price level (Pe) determined purely by private market forces, where MPB = MPC without government intervention.

Socially Optimal Equilibrium (Es / Qs, Ps)

The ideal production and consumption level (Qs) where total social welfare is maximized because MSB = MSC, achieving allocative efficiency.

Indirect Subsidy

A payment made by the government to producers or firms when producing a good or service, which lowers their marginal private cost of production.

Direct Subsidy

A subsidy, grant, or voucher given directly to consumers or households to purchase specific goods and services, which increases their marginal private benefit.

Free Provision

An extreme case of a subsidy where the government fully subsidises the entire cost of a product and provides it to consumers at zero price.

Direct Government Provision

Occurs when the government engages in the provision of a good directly (which does not necessarily mean the government produces the good itself, nor is it strictly free of charge).

Joint Provision

The government supplies a fixed quantity alongside private producers. Total supply is the horizontal sum of public and private supply. A lower market price can reduce private output, so the increase in total output is smaller than public output.

This branch isolates the market-supply effect. Public output is sold at the market price; public production costs are not specified. The original Qs is a reference only: a new social optimum and DWL require a model of public resource costs.

Legislation (Compulsory Consumption)

Rules or laws set by the government to force or ensure that a certain amount of goods with positive externalities are consumed or produced, backed by penalties or punitive measures.

Education & Campaigns

Government measures used to persuade consumers to voluntarily consume more of a good generating positive externalities by encouraging them to internalise the external benefits.
