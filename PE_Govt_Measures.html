Diagram Assumptions

This diagram illustrates positive externalities in consumption: MSB = MPB + MEB. External costs are assumed absent (MEC = 0), so MSC = MPC.

Glossary

MPB (Marginal Private Benefit)

The additional benefit enjoyed by the private consumer from consuming an extra unit of a good or service.

MPC (Marginal Private Cost)

The additional cost incurred by the private producer from producing an extra unit of a good or service.

MSC (Marginal Social Cost)

The total additional cost to society of producing an extra unit of output: MSC = MPC + MEC.

MSB (Marginal Social Benefit)

The total additional benefit to society of consuming an extra unit of output: MSB = MPB + MEB.

MEB (Marginal External Benefit)

The additional uncompensated benefit enjoyed by third parties outside the market transaction from consuming one extra unit.

Externality

An uncompensated cost or benefit affecting third parties outside a market transaction, arising from production or consumption.

Market Failure

A situation in which the free market fails to allocate resources efficiently, so net social benefit is not maximised.

Allocative Efficiency

An allocation of resources that maximises net social benefit. At an interior socially optimal output, marginal social benefit equals marginal social cost (MSB = MSC).

Internalisation Of Externalities

Bringing external costs or benefits into the incentives faced by decision makers, so their production or consumption decisions take account of these effects on third parties.

Deadweight Loss (DWL)

The loss of net social benefit relative to the socially optimal output.

The shaded area in this diagram shows the net social benefits forgone or net social costs incurred because output differs from the socially optimal level.

Market Equilibrium (Ee / Qe, Pe)

The output level (Qe) and price level (Pe) determined purely by private market forces, where MPB = MPC without government intervention.

Socially Optimal Output (Qs)

The output that maximises net social benefit. At an interior optimum, MSB = MSC. If MSC is at least MSB at every feasible positive quantity, zero output can be a boundary optimum without an intersection in the positive quadrant.

Indirect Subsidy

A payment made by the government to producers or firms when producing a good or service, which lowers their marginal private cost of production.

Direct Subsidy

A payment to consumers linked to purchasing the good. In this widget, it raises the effective willingness to pay received by producers by the subsidy amount; it does not itself change the underlying benefit from consumption.

Free Provision

A full producer subsidy lets consumers receive the good at zero price. Producers receive a reimbursement from the government. Varying MEB reveals excessive, socially optimal or insufficient provision. Free provision eliminates DWL only when its output coincides with Qs; it can otherwise improve or worsen welfare relative to the free market.

Direct Government Provision

Occurs when the government engages in the provision of a good directly (which does not necessarily mean the government produces the good itself, nor is it strictly free of charge).

Joint Provision

Definition: The government supplies a quantity of the good alongside private producers.

Effect In This Diagram: Total supply is the horizontal sum of public and private supply. A fixed public quantity shifts MPC* horizontally relative to MPC = MSC over the displayed range. A lower market price can reduce private output, so the increase in total output is smaller than public output.

Welfare Limitation: This branch isolates the market-supply effect. Public output is sold at the market price; public production costs are not specified. The original Qs is a reference only: a new social optimum and DWL require a model of public resource costs.

Other Measures
Legislation (Compulsory Consumption)

Rules or laws set by the government to force or ensure that a certain amount of goods with positive externalities are consumed or produced, backed by penalties or punitive measures.

Education & Campaigns

Information and persuasion intended to encourage consumption. This widget represents the response as an increase in perceived private benefit or willingness to pay.
