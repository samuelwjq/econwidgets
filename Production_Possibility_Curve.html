Glossary

Scarcity

The condition in which available resources are limited relative to unlimited wants, so not all wants can be satisfied.

Choice

The selection between competing alternatives made necessary by scarcity. Choosing one alternative involves forgoing the next best alternative.

Resource Allocation

The distribution of scarce resources among competing uses, determining what goods and services are produced and in what quantities. In this PPC diagram, different output combinations represent different allocations of resources.

Expand a term for its definition.

Production Possibility Curve (PPC)

A curve showing the various combinations of the maximum quantity of two outputs that an economy can produce within a specified period of time with all its resources fully and efficiently employed, assuming a particular state of technology.

Attainable Output Combinations

Output combinations located on or inside the PPC that can be achieved with the available resources and state of technology.

Unattainable Output Combinations

Output combinations located outside the PPC that cannot be achieved given the present amount of resources and state of technology, illustrating the concept of scarcity.

Opportunity Cost

Refers to the value of (or net benefit that could be derived from) the next best alternative forgone as a result of a decision made.

Rate of Opportunity Cost Incurred

Refers to the rate at which one good must be given up per unit of the other good gained. This is represented by the absolute size of the slope of the PPC.

Constant Opportunity Cost

Occurs when all units of the resource used are equally skilled and adaptable in producing both goods, resulting in a straight-line PPC.

Law of Increasing Opportunity Cost

Implies that as an economy produces more of one good, it has to sacrifice ever-increasing amounts of the other good because resources are usually not perfectly transferable, resulting in a PPC concave to the origin.

Unemployment

Refers to a condition in which some available resources are not being used in the production of goods and services, placing the economy at a point inside the PPC.

Underemployment

Refers to a condition that arises when resources, although being employed in production, are not fully utilised to their full potential.

Inefficiency

A condition in which resources are engaged in production but are not operating at their potential, causing the economy to produce inside the PPC.

Productive Efficiency

Achieved when the maximum output is produced for a given amount of inputs, or a given output is produced at the least possible cost; this is satisfied at any point along the PPC.

Allocative Efficiency

Achieved when society produces and consumes a combination of goods that maximises its welfare. For given societal preferences, this is a particular combination on the PPC; the PPC alone does not identify it.

Actual Economic Growth

An increase in the real output of a country, represented by a movement from a point inside the PPC to a point closer to or on the PPC.

Negative Actual Growth

A decrease in the real output of a country, illustrated here by a movement from A to B away from the PPC and towards the origin.

Potential Economic Growth

The rise in the potential of the economy to produce more goods and services, represented graphically when the PPC shifts outward away from the origin.

Pure Reallocation Along The PPC

A movement from A to B along the same PPC. Both output combinations have all resources fully and efficiently employed. More of one output requires sacrificing the other; productive capacity does not change.

Productive Capacity

The maximum output combinations that an economy can produce with its available resources and state of technology, within a specified period of time.

Capital Goods (Producer Goods)

Man-made aids to production that increase the potential to produce more goods and services in the future.

Consumer Goods

Goods bought for final consumption, usually by individual households.
