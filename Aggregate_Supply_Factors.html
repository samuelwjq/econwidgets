Glossary

Aggregate Supply (AS)

The total real output producers are willing and able to supply at each general price level over a period of time. The Keynesian AS curve has horizontal, intermediate and vertical sections.

Movement along AS

A change between price–output combinations on the same, unchanged AS curve. Points A and B are not necessarily equilibria. On the horizontal section price is unchanged as output varies; on the vertical section output is unchanged as price varies.

Short-Run Aggregate Supply (SRAS)

Production costs affect the price level at which producers supply output. Higher costs shift SRAS up; lower costs shift SRAS down, holding productive capacity constant.

Long-Run Aggregate Supply (LRAS)

Full-employment output reflects productive capacity. More resources or higher productivity increase this capacity; fewer resources or lower productivity reduce it.

Cost of Labour

Labour costs affect unit production costs, holding productivity constant. Higher costs shift SRAS up; lower costs shift it down.

Price of Raw Materials

Higher raw-material prices raise production costs and shift SRAS up; lower prices shift it down.

Exchange Rate

An increase means appreciation of the domestic currency. Imported inputs become cheaper in domestic currency, reducing production costs. Depreciation reverses this effect. Only this imported-input channel is shown; AD is held fixed.

Productivity

Output per unit of input. Higher productivity lowers unit production costs and raises productive capacity, shifting SRAS down and LRAS right. Lower productivity reverses both effects.

Quantity of Resources

The resources available for production. An increase raises productive capacity; a decrease reduces it. In this widget this factor changes LRAS only.

Aggregate Demand (AD)

Total planned expenditure on domestic final goods and services at each general price level. AD = C + I + G + (X − M). Optional AD is fixed to isolate AS changes.
