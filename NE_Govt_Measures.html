Diagram Assumptions

This diagram illustrates negative externalities in production: MSC = MPC + MEC. External benefits are assumed absent (MEB = 0), so MSB = MPB.

Government Policies

MPB (Marginal Private Benefit)

The additional benefit enjoyed by the private consumer from consuming an extra unit of a good or service.

MPC (Marginal Private Cost)

The additional cost incurred by the private producer from producing an extra unit of a good or service.

MSC (Marginal Social Cost)

The total additional cost to society of producing an extra unit of output: MSC = MPC + MEC.

MSB (Marginal Social Benefit)

The total additional benefit to society of consuming an extra unit of output: MSB = MPB + MEB.

MEC (Marginal External Cost)

The additional uncompensated cost inflicted on third parties outside the market transaction from producing one extra unit.

Externality

An uncompensated cost or benefit affecting third parties outside a market transaction, arising from production or consumption.

Market Failure

A situation in which the free market fails to allocate resources efficiently, so net social benefit is not maximised.

Allocative Efficiency

An allocation of resources that maximises net social benefit. At an interior socially optimal output, marginal social benefit equals marginal social cost (MSB = MSC).

Internalisation Of Externalities

Bringing external costs or benefits into the incentives faced by decision makers, so their production or consumption decisions take account of these effects on third parties.

Tax

A per-unit tax on a good generating negative externalities. It raises the effective marginal cost faced by producers, shifting MPC upward to MPC + Tax. In this model, a corrective tax equal to MEC at the socially optimal output aligns private incentives with social costs.

Quota on Output

A legally binding restriction capping output at a maximum quantity. It prevents market over-production beyond the specified limit.

Ban (a quota of zero)

A complete ban prevents production and consumption: no output. Its welfare effect depends on the external cost. A ban can remove harmful units but also prevent units for which MSB exceeds MSC.

Compare its DWL with the free-market DWL at the same MEC. Zero output is efficient when MSC ≥ MSB for every feasible positive quantity; this can be a boundary optimum without an intersection in the positive quadrant.

Quota On Pollutant

A legally imposed maximum level of emissions. This widget represents compliance as a reduction in external harm per unit of output (MEC). A zero-pollutant setting is assumed to remove the modelled external cost. In practice, compliance may also involve abatement costs or reduced output; those mechanisms are distinct from the MEC reduction shown here.

Research & Development (R&D) For Cleaner Technology

Government support for research and development of cleaner technology. Successful adoption is assumed in this widget to reduce external harm per unit of output (MEC), shifting MSC towards MPC. Funding alone does not guarantee this effect.

Education & Campaigns

Information and persuasion intended to encourage voluntary adoption of less harmful practices. In this widget, adoption is assumed to raise private costs. Compulsory requirements are a form of regulation.

Socially Optimal Output (Qs)

The output that maximises net social benefit. At an interior optimum, MSB = MSC. If MSC is at least MSB at every feasible positive quantity, zero output can be a boundary optimum without an intersection in the positive quadrant.

Deadweight Loss (DWL)

The loss of net social benefit relative to the socially optimal output.

The shaded area in this diagram shows the net social benefits forgone or net social costs incurred because output differs from the socially optimal level.
