Government Policies

Diagram assumptions & definitions

This diagram illustrates negative externalities in production: MSC = MPC + MEC, with no external benefits (MSB = MPB).

MPB: the additional benefit to the consumer from consuming an additional unit. MPC: the additional cost to the producer of producing an additional unit.

Tax

A per-unit tax placed on a good generating negative externalities. It increases private costs (shifting MPC upward to MPC + Tax). When correctly calibrated, internalises the external cost and reduces production to Qs.

Quota on Output

A legally binding restriction capping output at a maximum quantity. It prevents market over-production beyond the specified limit.

Ban (a quota of zero)

A complete ban prevents production and consumption: no output. Its welfare effect depends on the external cost. A ban can remove harmful units but also prevent units for which MSB exceeds MSC.

Compare its DWL with the free-market DWL at the same MEC. Zero output is efficient when MSC ≥ MSB for every feasible positive quantity; this can be a boundary optimum without an intersection in the positive quadrant.

Quota on Pollutant

A mandatory legal ceiling placed directly on emissions. Restricting allowable pollutants reduces the marginal external cost (MEC). A strict zero-pollutant quota removes external costs entirely (no external cost).

Research & Development (R&D)

Government funding for cleaner technologies reduces MEC directly. This shifts the MSC curve downward closer to MPC, creating a new social optimum at higher output (Qs').

Education & Campaigns

Initiatives forcing producers or consumers to adopt mitigation practices, raising private costs (MPC) closer to social costs.

Social Optimum (Qs / Qs')

The allocatively efficient point where social welfare is maximized (MSB = MSC).

Deadweight Loss (DWL)

The loss of net social benefit caused when production exceeds or falls short of the socially optimal level (shaded red triangle).
