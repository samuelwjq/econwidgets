Glossary

Cross-Price Elasticity Of Demand

Cross-price elasticity of demand (XED) measures the degree of responsiveness of demand for one good to a change in the price of another good, ceteris paribus.

Complementary Goods

Goods where a negative XED means that a rise (fall) in the price of one good will lead to a fall (rise) in the demand for another good, ceteris paribus.

A larger absolute XED indicates greater responsiveness and a stronger complementary relationship. This widget’s weak and strong cases use an absolute XED of one as a diagram convention.

Unrelated Goods

Goods with zero XED: a change in the price of one good does not lead to a change in demand for the other good, ceteris paribus.

Substitute Goods

Goods where a positive XED means that a rise in the price of one good will lead to a rise in the demand for another good, ceteris paribus.

A larger absolute XED indicates greater responsiveness and a stronger substitute relationship. This widget’s weak and strong cases use an absolute XED of one as a diagram convention.

Demand

Refers to the entire relationship between price and quantity demanded, as represented by the entire demand curve (or the quantity of a good or service which consumers are willing and able to buy at different price levels, over a specific time period, ceteris paribus).

Supply

Refers to the entire relationship between price and quantity supplied, as represented by the entire supply curve.

Market Equilibrium

Occurs when quantity demanded equals quantity supplied (Qdd = Qss), resulting in a situation where there is no tendency for consumers and producers to change their willingness to buy and sell.
