Glossary

Imperfect Information

When buyers or sellers have incomplete or inaccurate information relevant to their decisions. Here, they mis-estimate private costs or benefits.

Marginal Private Benefit (MPB)

The additional benefit to the consumer from consuming one more unit of a good or service.

Marginal Private Cost (MPC)

The additional private cost borne by consumers from consuming one more unit. In this widget, MPC represents the consumer-side private costs relevant to the decision, including perceived harms.

Actual And Perceived Values

Actual values reflect the true costs or benefits. Perceived values are those decision-makers believe they face and use when making choices.

Marginal Social Benefit (MSB)

The additional benefit to society from one more unit of consumption. With no external benefits in this model, MSB equals actual MPB.

Marginal Social Cost (MSC)

The additional cost to society from one more unit of consumption. With no external costs in this model, MSC equals actual MPC.

Market Equilibrium

The outcome where perceived marginal private benefit equals perceived marginal private cost. The curve which is correctly estimated has identical actual and perceived values.

Socially Efficient Output

The output where MSB = MSC, maximising society’s net benefit. It is determined by actual costs and benefits.

Overconsumption And Underconsumption

Overconsumption occurs when market output exceeds socially efficient output. Underconsumption occurs when market output is below socially efficient output.

Deadweight Loss (DWL)

The loss of society’s net benefit from consuming above or below the socially efficient quantity. It is measured between actual MSB and MSC over the misallocated units.

Diagram Assumptions

No externalities, market power or other distortions. Only one private curve is mis-estimated at a time, with the same initial error size in each case. MPC is interpreted as a consumer-side private cost. Information policies correct perceptions; fiscal policies alter incentives without correcting beliefs. Tax revenue and subsidy expenditure are transfers; implementation costs and financing distortions are excluded. The information error is a constant vertical gap, so curves shift in parallel. Curves are straight lines with equal axis scales. Output is traded at the perceived market equilibrium.

Education & Campaigns

Help consumers understand actual private costs or benefits. Improved understanding moves perceived values towards actual values.

Accurate Information Through Legislation

Disclosure requirements, labels and warnings provide accurate information. The correction slider represents how far consumers incorporate it into their decisions.

Compulsory Consumption

Requires consumption at the socially efficient quantity in an underconsumption case. It changes consumption directly while leaving perceptions unchanged. Full compliance is assumed.

Taxes And Subsidies

A tax discourages consumption; a subsidy encourages it. They change effective private incentives rather than beliefs. Excessive intervention or the wrong intervention creates deadweight loss.

Tax Revenue And Subsidy Expenditure

The per-unit tax or subsidy multiplied by the quantity consumed after intervention. These transfers do not themselves change actual MSB or MSC in this model.
