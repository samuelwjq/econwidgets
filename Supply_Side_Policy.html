Glossary

Aggregate Demand (AD)

Total planned expenditure on domestic final goods and services at each general price level. AD = C + I + G + (X − M).

Aggregate Supply (AS)

Total real output producers are willing and able to supply at each general price level over a period of time. The Keynesian curve has horizontal, intermediate and vertical sections.

Macroeconomic Equilibrium

The intersection of AD and AS determines the equilibrium general price level and real national income.

Short-Run Aggregate Supply (SRAS)

Lower production costs increase SRAS, represented by a downwards shift. Higher costs decrease SRAS, represented by an upwards shift.

Long-Run Aggregate Supply (LRAS)

Productive capacity determines full-employment output. An increase shifts LRAS rightwards; a decrease shifts it leftwards.

Initial State of the Economy

On horizontal SRAS there is spare capacity. The intermediate section represents increasing capacity constraints. On vertical LRAS the economy is at full-employment output.

Supply-Side Policy

Measures intended to improve the economy’s ability to produce goods and services. In this diagram, aggregate demand is held fixed.

Interventionist Policies

The government acts directly, for example by providing grants or regulating factor prices.

Market-Oriented Policies

The government uses market incentives, such as increased competition, to improve efficiency and resource allocation.

Productivity

Output per unit of input. Labour productivity is output per unit of labour, such as output per hour worked.

Cost of Production (COP)

The costs firms incur in producing output. Lower unit costs can increase short-run aggregate supply.
