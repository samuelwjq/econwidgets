Glossary

Change In Demand

A change in the quantity demanded at every price caused by a non-price determinant of demand, represented by a shift of the demand curve, ceteris paribus.

Change In Supply

A change in the quantity supplied at every price caused by a non-price determinant of supply, represented by a shift of the supply curve, ceteris paribus.

Change In Quantity Demanded

A change in the quantity consumers are willing and able to buy caused by a change in the good’s own price, represented by a movement along the same demand curve, ceteris paribus.

Change In Quantity Supplied

A change in the quantity producers are willing and able to offer for sale caused by a change in the good’s own price, represented by a movement along the same supply curve, ceteris paribus.

Demand

Refers to the entire relationship between price and quantity demanded, as represented by the entire demand curve (or the quantity of a good or service which consumers are willing and able to buy at different price levels, over a specific time period, ceteris paribus).

Quantity Demanded

Refers to a particular quantity that the consumer is willing and able to buy at a particular price, as reflected by a given point on the demand curve.

Supply

Refers to the entire relationship between price and quantity supplied, as represented by the entire supply curve.

Quantity Supplied

Refers to a particular quantity offered for sale at a particular price, as reflected by a point on a given supply curve.

Shortage

A market condition where quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied (Qdd > Qss), meaning more is demanded than producers are willing and able to supply at that price.

Surplus

A market condition where quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded (Qss > Qdd), meaning more is supplied than consumers are willing and able to demand at that price.

Market Equilibrium

Occurs when quantity demanded equals quantity supplied (Qdd = Qss), resulting in a situation where there is no tendency for consumers and producers to change their willingness to buy and sell.

Consumer Surplus (CS)

The difference between the maximum price a consumer is willing and able to pay and the price that he actually pays.

How To Find The Area: At the current equilibrium, shade the area below the current demand curve and above the equilibrium price P1, from zero to Q1. This represents total consumer surplus in the market.

Producer Surplus (PS)

The net benefit enjoyed by a producer, represented graphically by the area above the supply curve and below the selling/equilibrium price.

How To Find The Area: At the current equilibrium, shade the area above the current supply curve and below the equilibrium price P1, from zero to Q1. This represents total producer surplus in the market.
