Glossary

Aggregate Demand (AD)

Total planned expenditure on domestic final goods and services at each general price level. AD = C + I + G + (X − M).

Aggregate Supply (AS)

Total real output producers are willing and able to supply at each general price level over a period of time. The Keynesian curve has horizontal, intermediate and vertical sections.

Macroeconomic Equilibrium

The intersection of AD and AS determines the equilibrium general price level and real national income.

Multiplier Effect

An autonomous spending change generates income, which leads to further rounds of induced consumption. Leakages through saving, taxation and imports limit successive rounds. A larger multiplier produces a larger total spending response for the same initial change.

The Zero–Large control is an illustrative effect-size scale, not a numerical estimate of the expenditure multiplier. Zero deliberately switches off the modelled spending response. The equilibrium income change also depends on AS and changes in prices.

Initial Spending Change

The autonomous spending increase or decrease that starts the process. Its size is distinct from the size of the multiplier. The widget shows the combined AD shift, rather than animating individual multiplier rounds.

Short-Run Aggregate Supply (SRAS)

Lower production costs increase SRAS, represented by a downwards shift. Higher costs decrease SRAS, represented by an upwards shift. This control holds productive capacity fixed.

Long-Run Aggregate Supply (LRAS)

Productive capacity determines full-employment output. An increase shifts LRAS rightwards; a decrease shifts it leftwards. This control holds the horizontal section's price level fixed.

Initial State of the Economy

On horizontal SRAS there is spare capacity. The intermediate section represents increasing capacity constraints. On vertical LRAS the economy is at full-employment output.

Simultaneous Changes

AD and AS can change together. Their relative sizes determine the final effects on prices and real national income. One variable may be unchanged when opposing effects offset each other.
