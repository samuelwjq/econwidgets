Glossary

Perfectly Price Inelastic Demand

|PED| = 0. Quantity demanded does not respond to a change in the good’s own price, ceteris paribus. The curve is vertical.

Perfectly Price Elastic Demand

|PED| approaches infinity. The curve is horizontal: any amount can be demanded at the given price. An arbitrarily small price change causes an unbounded proportional quantity response in this limiting case, ceteris paribus.

Price Elasticity Of Demand

The price elasticity of demand (PED) measures the degree of responsiveness of quantity demanded of a good to a change in the price of the good itself, ceteris paribus.

This widget uses the absolute value of PED, |PED|, for classification.

Price Inelastic Demand

If the |PED| value is less than 1, the demand for the good is price inelastic, i.e. a proportionate change in price leads to a less than proportionate change in quantity demanded, ceteris paribus.

Unit Elastic Demand

If the |PED| value is equal to 1, the demand for the good is unitary price elastic, i.e. a percentage change in price is equal in size to the percentage change in quantity demanded.

Price Elastic Demand

If the |PED| value is greater than 1, demand for the good is price elastic, i.e. a proportionate change in price leads to a more than proportionate change in quantity demanded, ceteris paribus.

Total Revenue

Total Revenue (firms) = Price (P) × Quantity (Q) = Total Expenditure (consumers).

Price Effect

The gain or loss in total revenue from the price change on the initial quantity Q0.

Price Effect = Q0 × (P1 − P0).

Quantity Effect

The gain or loss in total revenue from the quantity change valued at the new price P1.

Quantity Effect = P1 × (Q1 − Q0).

Demand

Refers to the entire relationship between price and quantity demanded, as represented by the entire demand curve (or the quantity of a good or service which consumers are willing and able to buy at different price levels, over a specific time period, ceteris paribus).

Supply

Refers to the entire relationship between price and quantity supplied, as represented by the entire supply curve.

Market Equilibrium

Occurs when quantity demanded equals quantity supplied (Qdd = Qss), resulting in a situation where there is no tendency for consumers and producers to change their willingness to buy and sell.
